# Readers Still Catch Generalizations the Quote Gate Misses

Authors: Klara Novak. Human Review After Automatic Citation Checks, 2025.

After the quote gate and the passage-id check, we gave the surviving claims to two readers. Each reader saw the claim, the copied quote, and the cited passage. They marked a claim as a generalization when the quote was present and the claim still said more than the passage.

Of 80 claims the gate had called supported, the readers agreed that 11 were generalizations. Typical additions were "in general," "across sciences," or a second number taken from a different paper. The gate had no signal for those words, because the required quote was still a contiguous span of the cited passage.

Automatic citation checks remove claims that cannot point at a retrieved sentence. They leave claims that point at a true sentence and then widen it. A retained quote is evidence that the sentence was copied, not evidence that the claim stopped at that sentence.
