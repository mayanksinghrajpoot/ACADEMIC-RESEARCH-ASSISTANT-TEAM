# Passage Identifiers as an Audit Trail for Literature Claims

Authors: Priya Singh and Henrik Berg. Evidence Review Methods, 2024.

A literature summary is hard to audit when citations point only at a paper title. Reviewers need the sentence. We attached a stable passage identifier to every chunk of a fixed paper set and required each claim in a draft summary to name one or more of those identifiers.

The identifier did not make a claim true. It made the check mechanical. A claim whose identifiers were missing, unknown, or absent from the retrieved set could be rejected before anyone debated the science. In a review of 120 draft claims, 19 failed that identifier check. Most of those 19 named a paper that existed in the corpus but quoted a sentence from a different chunk than the one retrieved for the question.

Where the identifier matched a retrieved passage, a second reader compared the claim text to that passage. Agreement between readers was higher than in a title-only citation scheme, because both readers looked at the same sentences. Passage identifiers are an audit trail. They do not replace entailment, and they do not license a claim that merely shares a topic with the passage. They do stop a summary from keeping a statement that cannot be traced to the retrieved text.
